It will find what you want read

Three ways, tried in that order, so you almost never have to think about it.

  1. Just press Ctrl twice.

    In ChatGPT or Claude it finds the newest answer by itself. Nothing to highlight, nothing to aim at.

  2. Not what you wanted? Select it and press Ctrl twice.

    Any text, in any app, on any page. An email, a PDF, a paragraph halfway down a long answer.

  3. Nothing selected? It reads whatever you copied last.

    So the thing you already have in hand is never more than two taps away.

Whichever one it lands on, it starts speaking in ~0.3 seconds.

What makes it different

The first word lands in 0.3 seconds
And it stays that fast whether the answer is four lines or four thousand words. Every other reader you have tried makes you wait first.
It sounds like a person, not a screen reader
Headings pause. Tables make sense out loud. Formulae come out as spoken maths. Acronyms are said properly, so you get "you eye" and not "U. I." You can listen to a whole answer without wincing.
Code gets explained, not spelled out
A thirty line function becomes one sentence telling you what it does, so you keep listening instead of reaching for stop.
54 voices, or invent your own
British and American, male and female. Blend any two into one that is yours, change it whenever you like, and hear it straight away.

Installing it

  1. Open the disk image and drag Hark into Applications.

    The window shows the app and the folder side by side.

  2. Open Hark. macOS will refuse, and say Apple cannot check it for malware.

    Click Done. This is expected, and it is what happens to every app not signed with a paid Apple certificate.

  3. Go to System Settings, Privacy & Security, and scroll to Security.

    There is a line about Hark being blocked, with an Open Anyway button next to it. Click it and confirm with your password or Touch ID.

  4. Turn Hark on under Accessibility, in the same Privacy & Security settings.

    This is the one permission it needs, and usually the only one. If tapping Ctrl twice still does nothing afterwards, a few Macs also want Hark switched on under Input Monitoring.

Questions

Why does my Mac say it cannot check Hark for malware?

Because we have not paid Apple the 99 dollars a year for a developer certificate yet. That fee buys a signature, not a safety check, and the warning says only that the signature is missing. We would rather put the money into the product during beta and be honest about the warning than hide it.

Will I have to grant Accessibility again after an update?

Yes, for now, and we are sorry about it. macOS ties that permission to an app's signature, and an unsigned build gets a new one each time. This stops the day we buy the certificate above.

What does Hark send anywhere?

The speech happens on your Mac, so nothing you listen to leaves it. One thing does go out: when an answer contains a code block, that block is sent to our server to get back a one sentence description of what it does. You can turn that off in the dashboard, and everything else keeps working. We also count how often reads happen, with no text attached, to know whether people come back.

What stops working if your server goes down?

Code descriptions, and nothing else. Everything else keeps working exactly as it does now, including offline. A code block gets announced rather than described, and the read never pauses waiting for it.

Does it work on an Intel Mac, or on Windows?

Not yet. This build is Apple silicon only. Windows is a real plan and most of the work carries over, though the part that finds the newest answer has to be rewritten against a different system interface, so it is weeks away rather than days.

Why is the download so large?

The voice model is 310 MB of it. It is the price of the voice running on your machine instead of in someone's cloud, and of it working on a plane. We are looking at a smaller version of the model.

Is it going to stay free?

The beta is free and has no account. A paid version is coming, and when it does we will say clearly what changes before anything does.
